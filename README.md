<h1>To Raise The Rays</h1>

<h2> Play as Ra, the God of the Sun, as he completes his daily task of sailing the sun around the world. Each day, or stage, consists of both a day and a night section. During the day, you collect light and regain your health in order to prepare for the night stage. You can also collect upgrades for both yourself and your boat. During this time, you travel through Duat, the underworld, and must fight off onslaughts of enemies while avoiding obstacles. Sometimes, you even face Apophis, a giant serpent, in a boss fight.<br><br>
To complete these tasks, you are able to control your boat freely and shoot projectiles upwards. Your goal is to light up the world, so your health depends on the intensity of the light around you. The more damage you take, the more difficult it is to see. In addition, the later it gets in the night, the more difficult the enemies become.</h2>

<p>This is the product of a semester-long group project for IGME 106: Data Structures and Algorithms. It is a single-player top down shoot-em-up style game that features a mix of normal enemies and boss fights following a set day-night cycle.</p>

<p>After completing the project, the group agreed that we were proud of how the hard difficulty seemed to remain well-balanced and kept players coming back for more, and that the visual cohesion was complimented in surveys given to play-testers. The overall game was generally well-regarded by our classmates.</p>
  
<p>Something that we wish went better, though, was a small bug related to the player position that occurred at a seemingly random frequency during the night section. We tried to fix it as much as possible, but its randomness made it difficult to fully solve. We also planned on including a mechanic known as the Fog of War, which was implemented but felt unnecessary with the progress already made by that point.</p>

<p>Our biggest takeaways from this project lay primarily in the realm of teamwork. The general consensus was that we should anticipate more intersection between roles, as otherwise branches can differ so severely it takes a long time to integrate all of the changes, and that deadlines should be more clearly enforced.</p>
